# Online-Retail-Customer-Behavior-Cancellation-Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer purchasing behaviour, sales performance, and order cancellation patterns for an online retail business.
The main objective was to understand the difference between one-time and repeat customers, determine how much revenue repeat customers contribute, and investigate whether cancellation behaviour changes as customers make more purchases.
The analysis was designed around the following business question:

Are repeat customers cancelling more orders simply because they make more purchases, or does cancellation behaviour increase as customer purchase frequency increases?

## Business Objectives

The analysis focused on:

- Understanding the distribution of one-time and repeat customers
- Comparing revenue generated by one-time and repeat customers
- Measuring cancelled orders by customer type
- Calculating cancellation rates
- Investigating cancellation behaviour across different purchase-frequency groups
- Identifying products and countries associated with cancellation activity
- Examining monthly cancellation trends
- Data Preparation

## The dataset was cleaned and prepared using Python and Pandas before being analyzed in Power BI.

## The data preparation process included:

- Handling missing product descriptions
- Matching missing descriptions using StockCode where possible
- Cleaning inconsistent product description values
- Preparing customer information for analysis
- Identifying cancelled transactions using the invoice number convention
- Preparing calculated fields and measures for customer segmentation and cancellation analysis
- Customer Purchasing Behaviour

## The analysis identified 8,082 customers.

Approximately 62% of customers were one-time customers, while approximately 38% were repeat customers.
This shows that the majority of customers made only one purchase during the period analyzed, while a smaller proportion returned to make additional purchases.
However, the revenue contribution of these groups was substantially different.
Repeat customers generated approximately £8.5 million in sales revenue, compared with approximately £2.2 million generated by one-time customers.
This means that although repeat customers represented a smaller share of the customer base, they generated considerably more revenue.
This highlights the importance of understanding the behaviour of repeat customers, particularly when cancellations are taken into account.

## Cancellation Behaviour

The analysis showed a strong concentration of cancelled orders among repeat customers.
Repeat customers generated approximately 3.6K cancelled orders, compared with approximately 0.2K cancelled orders from one-time customers.
This means that cancellation activity was overwhelmingly concentrated among repeat customers.
However, cancellation volume alone does not tell us whether repeat customers are actually more likely to cancel. Repeat customers make more purchases, giving them more opportunities to generate cancelled transactions.

## For this reason, the analysis also examined cancellation rates based on customer purchase frequency.
## Cancellation Rate by Purchase Frequency

Customers were grouped according to the number of purchases they made:

- 1 purchase
- 2 purchases
- 3-5 purchases
- 6-10 purchases
- 11+ purchases

The analysis showed that cancellation rates increased as purchase frequency increased.

Purchase Frequency	Cancellation Rate
- 1 purchase	4.22%
- 2 purchases	12.00%
- 3-5 purchases	14.80%
- 6-10 purchases	17.79%
- 11+ purchases	19.14%

Customers with one purchase had a cancellation rate of 4.22%, while customers with 11 or more purchases had the highest cancellation rate at 19.14%.
This represents a substantial difference in cancellation behaviour across purchase-frequency groups.
The finding suggests that cancellation behaviour should be monitored not only by customer type, but also by how frequently customers purchase.

## Product-Level Cancellation Analysis

The analysis also examined which products had the highest number of cancelled transactions.
Products such as Manual, Regency Cakestand 3 Tier, Postage, Jam Making Set With Jars, and Set of 3 Cake Tins Pantry Design appeared among the products with the highest cancellation volumes.
These products represent areas that could be investigated further to understand whether product-related, ordering, or fulfilment factors may be contributing to cancellations.
The analysis identifies where cancellation activity is concentrated, but does not establish the underlying cause of the cancellations.

## Cancellation Trends Over Time

Monthly cancellation activity also showed a noticeable increase toward the end of the year.
Cancelled orders increased from 260 in January to 472 in December, with November and December recording the highest cancellation volumes.
This suggests that the business should pay particular attention to cancellation activity during periods with high transaction volumes.
Further analysis of monthly cancellation rates would help determine whether the increase was caused by a higher number of overall orders or by an actual increase in the proportion of orders being cancelled.

## Key Insights

The analysis produced several key findings:

- One-time customers make up the majority of the customer base, representing approximately 62% of customers.
- Repeat customers generate substantially more revenue, contributing approximately £8.5M compared with £2.2M from one-time customers.
- Cancelled orders are heavily concentrated among repeat customers, with approximately 3.6K cancelled orders compared with approximately 0.2K from one-time customers.
- Cancellation rates increase with purchase frequency, rising from 4.22% among customers with one purchase to 19.14% among customers with 11+ purchases.
- Cancellation activity increased toward the end of the year, reaching 472 cancelled orders in December.
- Cancellation activity is concentrated among certain products, providing potential areas for further investigation.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the business should:

- Monitor cancellation rates among high-frequency customers rather than relying only on total cancellation counts.
- Investigate products with unusually high cancellation volumes.
- Examine the reasons behind cancellations among frequent and high-value customers.
- Monitor cancellation trends by country and customer segment.
- Compare monthly cancellation rates with total order volume to distinguish increased cancellations from increased sales activity.
- Develop strategies to retain one-time customers while also reducing cancellations among existing repeat customers.

This project demonstrates an end-to-end data analytics workflow, from data cleaning and preparation to customer segmentation, exploratory analysis, dashboard development, and business insight generation.
